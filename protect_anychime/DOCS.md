# Protect AnyChime documentation

## Chimes and speakers

Configure the app's verified Protect console connection in the web interface. Configure the local Protect management username/password in Home Assistant's app Configuration tab. Console certificates and accounts are installation-specific; credentials never belong in the source tree. The existing adoption, automatic-adoption option, native chime protocol, management ownership checks and ringtone upload/storage remain in use.

Available speakers are individual eligible `media_player` entities. HA group entities remain individual choices; AnyChime does not inspect `group_members` to decide chime membership. Discovery refreshes names, availability, platform and device metadata without changing fixed sets. Native UniFi devices, known incompatible routes and entities without PLAY_MEDIA capability are hidden. Missing previously selected entities remain visible as offline members.

A chime owns a stable key and an ordered member list in private `/data/chimes.json`. A first fresh chime may use the existing `primary` slot; additional chimes receive independent random keys. Member additions, detachments and moves update the registry atomically and never move or replace identity/ringtone directories. Removing the member currently used as a representative changes only the representative entity, not the chime key or its Protect pairing. Moving the final member into another existing chime requires explicit confirmation. One atomic registry write adds it to the destination and queues removal of the now-empty source, preserving the destination identity and settings.

Use drag handles, +, per-chime Add speaker, member ×, or keyboard pickup/drop. Membership changes remain provisional in the UI until the server accepts them. Failed operations restore the previous display. Chime-menu Remove and last-member × require confirmation, then call the existing local-account, pinned-session, owned-device deletion flow. A removal immediately unselects the speakers and pauses the virtual device. If Protect cannot confirm deletion yet, the app shows a pending-cleanup notice and retries with backoff. Saved identity/ringtone data is retained solely for that cleanup and erased only after confirmation. Removing a virtual chime from Protect also disables it in AnyChime. Automatic background deletion of historical unconfirmed identities is not performed.

The ingress-only membership endpoint is `POST /api/chimes/members`, with an explicit `confirmed: true`, `action` (`add`, `move` or `remove`), selected `entity_id`, and a destination `target_key` or source `key`. A null destination creates a chime. Last-member removal additionally requires `confirm_last: true`. Moving a final member to another chime requires `confirm_source_removal: true` and its current `source_key`, so a stale confirmation cannot remove a different source chime. A removal cannot be hidden inside an ordinary member move. Product image selection and console connection endpoints remain separate.

Upgrade migration is idempotent. Existing entries retain their keys, identity directories, ordered members, adoption and ringtone files. `/data/chime-schema.json` records completion. Unfinished legacy grouping transitions, conflicting old membership, or historical unconfirmed saved identities are held for review. The app does not replay old deletion instructions or mutate those identities. The user removes the affected chimes using the confirmed flow; `POST /api/chimes/migration/finish` explicitly finishes review once no held identity remains. An outstanding ordinary removal flag is retained unchanged and can be retried using Remove.

After completed migration, obsolete grouping preferences, microphone profiles, the synthetic click cache and the rendered latency cache are removed. Export omits them. Import validates older archive checksums before ignoring obsolete files; any unfinished transition is converted into explicit review state instead of disappearing. Normal HA app backups include all remaining `/data` state. The explicit export/import mechanism retains identities, current sets, trust, ringtones, manual offsets and migration review state.

The app uses host networking, an ingress/media listener on 48200 and per-virtual-device management forwarding. Network permissions are NET_ADMIN and NET_RAW. Dedicated nftables rules and journals are owned and cleaned up by the app; other applications' rules are not targets. HA OS has been exercised live; Supervised/Debian and different Docker/network backends require their own validation. No source release includes runtime identity, account, certificate or audio material.

## Timing

Timing is available for a chime with at least two configured members. Each member has a 0–500 ms slider and Save. Test requests playback only after an explicit click, at 10% by default. All normal Protect test ownership/adoption checks remain in effect. Editing or saving does not play sound. Saved offsets apply to doorbell rings as well as tests.

Manual values are private in `/data/latency-delays.json`, keyed by HA device ID, falling back to `entity_id` for players without a device. Existing values survive upgrade and export/import. Offline members retain their saved values. Two entities for the same HA device share its stored offset. A single effective playback target ignores offsets, even if more than one configured member collapses onto that target.

The backend preflights all selected members, filters off/standby/unavailable/unknown/muted players and computes effective output requests. A zero Protect volume performs no HA playback. Announcements retain `announce: true` and requested `extra.volume`; that extra volume is integration-specific. Normal playback checks or sets volume before sending media. Existing consistent music groups deduplicate to their reported coordinator. Sonos AudioClip requests remain per speaker. Normal Sonos group playback deduplicates as a group; incompatible mixed announcement capability in one Sonos music group fails before audio.

Effective requests run concurrently. Every repetition registers a fresh batch. For two or more requests, the URL query contains `ac_sync=<batch>.<member>` with random opaque identifiers. Offsets are stored in the server batch and never accepted from URL parameters. The expected count is per output token, so a shared proxy IP does not collapse distinct members. Unknown, expired, duplicate, missing or wrong-path tokens do not wait or add arrivals.

Only the first non-probe GET starting at byte zero waits, before headers. HEAD, tiny probes and later ranges bypass the barrier. One common release epoch is set when all members arrive or the one-second deadline expires. An output waits until that epoch plus its saved offset, with no more than 1.5 seconds of requested waiting in total. Late wakeups do not accumulate a new full offset. Batches expire and their count is bounded; capacity exhaustion retains plain original media delivery without a new barrier. Failed HA calls release pending waiters without retrying audio.

No ringtone is rendered or transcoded by AnyChime. The original stored MP3 is served with its usual range support. Repetitions remain separate requests after original duration + 50 ms; there is no combined-repeat file or alternative playback mode. A compact status summary reports expected/distinct downloads, arrival spread and release reason, with no IPs, tokens or URLs.

Download timing does not prove audible alignment. Players may buffer differently, proxy or cache requests, or perform transcoding. Grouped outputs cannot be individually delayed behind a single HA group/coordinator request. When that group participates with another output, its coordinator's saved delay is used if selected, otherwise the first selected member's. Previously calibrated numbers can be retained while producing different audible results from the old file-silence method. Real listening verification is required; no automated test here plays on real speakers.

## Partial playback failures and security

A failed state lookup, volume request or playback request affects only its effective output. Independent successful outputs continue their remaining repetitions. Failed members are excluded for the remainder of that ring, without retry or fallback playback; a new ring can try them again. A failed shared coordinator excludes the selected members represented by that request. Status reports `partial` when some outputs succeeded, with per-member outcomes and accepted-request counts. HA acceptance does not prove audible playback.

All interface and administrative API requests require the actual Supervisor ingress peer, including read-only status and discovery. Loopback and forwarded-address headers cannot authorize access. Speaker media downloads remain intentionally separate from ingress authorization. The enforced AppArmor policy limits file writes and executable paths while retaining the network capabilities needed for virtual chimes. See [security details](../docs/SECURITY.md).

## Chime setup status

With automatic adoption enabled and a local Protect account configured, new chimes show blue **Adopting in Protect**. Once adopted and connected, an unpaired chime shows orange **Configure chime in Protect**. Pair it with a doorbell in Protect and choose its sound, volume and repetitions there. Green **Active as chime** requires a confirmed doorbell pairing and a live chime connection; default sound/volume settings count as configured. This status does not prove acoustic playback.

Pairing is read from Protect for verified installation-owned identities approximately every thirty seconds, with up to another interface refresh interval before the label changes. Missing fields stay unknown. A temporary failed refresh retains the last confirmed pairing for up to five minutes while the live chime connection is monitored separately. A warning requires at least three consecutive failures spanning ninety seconds, or evidence older than five minutes. Failed checks retry after 15, 30 and then 60 seconds; successful checks reset the failure episode. No doorbell identifiers or settings are added to the UI. Automatic adoption disabled keeps the orange **Adopt chime in Protect** instruction. Offline, migration and removal states take precedence.

Warnings and errors use orange throughout the interface. Destructive Remove remains a red action. Unknown configuration states do not imply an error; confirmed check failures and exhausted adoption attempts refer to app logs, where a bounded explanation is recorded without credentials or raw controller responses. Configuration logs use safe categories such as timeout, certificate, authentication, network and HTTP status without raw responses or credentials. Repeated identical transient warnings are limited to one per five minutes; persistent failure and subsequent recovery are logged separately.

## Moving from the previous app ID

Home Assistant identifies an app by its internal slug, not its visible name. Version 0.4.7 uses `protect_anychime` in the `protect_anychime/` repository folder. Earlier installations use `unifi_anychime`; the renamed app installs separately and does not automatically inherit that installation's `/data` directory. An ordinary HA backup still restores the original app ID.

1. Make a Home Assistant backup of the existing app. Keep it private: saved identities, keys, accounts and recordings belong to your installation.
2. In the existing app's interface, use **Move an existing installation → Pause bridge & export backup**. This pauses its native bridge. Stop the old app in HA before starting the renamed one.
3. Install the renamed app. Copy the local Protect account settings from the old app's HA Configuration tab if you use management or automatic adoption. Do not add speakers or establish new identities yet.
4. Start the new app, open its web interface and import the exported backup into this fresh installation. Restart the new app after importing.
5. Confirm that the original chimes and speaker sets return and connect to Protect. The backup preserves native device identities, controller trust, received ringtones and manual timing. Earlier exports may require the management connection to be verified again; newer exports also include its saved certificate trust.
6. Keep the old app stopped until verification is complete. You can then uninstall the old installation. Never run both copies of the same chime identities together.

Switching between a local app and a GitHub repository app can also change HA's app ID prefix. Use the same transfer process. Export/import does not copy Supervisor account options: configure the local Protect username/password and automatic-adoption preference in the new app's HA Configuration tab.

## Compact chime cards and action feedback

The chime actions menu stays attached to its ⋯ button and scrolls with the page. It opens below the button, or above it when there is not enough room. Clicking outside or pressing Escape closes it; scrolling leaves it open.

Adding or moving a speaker opens the receiving chime so its new membership is visible. Chime cards start collapsed on every page load, return visit and restored page. Only the title, expansion arrow and actions menu remain visible. Expand/collapse choices stay in memory through background status polls; they are not stored as a preference. A collapsed heading accepts dragged speakers. **Add speaker** also offers online speakers already assigned to another chime. Moving its last speaker requires an in-app confirmation explaining that the empty source is removed while the destination keeps its identity, pairing, ringtones and settings. HA music groups remain unchanged.

Remove and × work during preparation, adoption, offline conditions and management outages. They immediately remove membership locally; an unused chime with no saved identity can be discarded without a Protect request. Existing identities become inactive cleanup records. Only these explicit requests are automatically retried against their original console, with authenticated identity ownership checks and confirmed absence/deletion before final cleanup. Historical unconfirmed records are not automatically deleted. A visible pending-cleanup notice explains when Protect or account setup is still needed.

Actions show immediate progress, prevent duplicate submissions, and report completion or an orange error in a viewport-visible message. Membership errors are also shown on the affected card. Dialogs use in-app confirmation, with an explicit Cancel, rather than browser confirmation prompts. Refreshing speakers, checking/saving a console connection, timing requests, product-information saves and explicit test requests show progress. Tests remain opt-in at the requested volume; no opening, rendering, collapsing or saving operation starts audio.

Console certificate confirmation authorizes the native chime connection; the local Protect account in HA app Configuration authorizes management operations. Both are required for automatic adoption. Temporary rate limits, network failures and server errors retry with backoff without consuming the three adoption attempts. A rate limit pauses adoption across all chimes and defers configuration polling during the cooldown.

Explicit management actions log their operation, failure category and HTTP status without account details or request bodies. An upstream test failure does not necessarily mean playback was not started; sound tests are never automatically retried. The chime menu is a non-modal panel inside the app page, so the underlying page stays usable and scrollable. Removal confirmations are shown centred over Home Assistant.

While the app is open, confirmed Protect removals, adoption, doorbell configuration and sound-setting changes appear as notifications. Removals arrive through the native connection; configuration changes appear after a successful management poll. Initial observations, page revisits and bridge restarts do not replay old events. Notifications are held only in bounded runtime memory.

A newly created chime can take about a minute to appear in Protect. Discovery and adoption timing depend on the console and network; the app answers Protect discovery probes as soon as the virtual chime is ready. Adding a speaker to an existing chime does not require adopting another chime.
